Round 7 — The whole error is three numbers per
neuron: how to compute them better, cheaply
Answer to the Round-7 brief · bias-free ReLU MLP, n = 1024, depth 16 · estimator V29 (factorised K = 3 chain,
260 u) · all FLOP counts in units u = n³ ≈ 2³¹ FLOPs · all falsifiers stated for the stored per-layer arrays
Headline. The three channels are not equally worth fixing, and they are not three
independent problems — the cost profile says so. Variance (−47 %) is a smooth, low-
dimensional, exactly-derivable missing closure term: the random 4.2e-5 per entry is
the second-order (Edgeworth g³) term in the post-ReLU covariance that the chain
drops. That is roughly +0.30 u/layer (≈ 40 u total, 15 % of the current budget) to
recover most of the −47 %. κ₄ diagonal (−30 %) is a genuinely fourth-order tensor; the
(2,1,1)+(1,1,1,1) content it needs is not obtainable from any O(n³) object, but a rank-32
skew-embedded surrogate gets the per-neuron pattern for ≈ +0.18 u/layer. Sampling
(Q3) is not a competitive route at 10⁴–10⁵ samples for κ₄, but it is the cheapest honest
way to validate Q1's new closure term and to build the κ₄ control variate. Run Q1's g³-
term experiment first.
0. Where the error actually lives (why the three channels separate)
Let z⁽ˡ⁾ be pre-activations, h⁽ˡ⁾ = relu(z⁽ˡ⁾). The prediction is μ⁽ᴸ⁾ = E[h⁽ᴸ⁾] and the
score is MSE_L · max(0.1, FLOPs/2⁴¹). From the oracle table, the error budget is
multiplicative down the mean recursion with per-layer gain g_l ∈ [0.65,0.96], so a per-layer
fresh error ε_l contributes to the final MSE roughly (∏_{k>l} g_k)² ε_l² ≈ (0.8)^{L−l}
ε_l². Deeper layers dominate; the three oracle ratios (0.533 / 0.699 / 0.858) are therefore final-
layer attributions, and the per-layer fresh-error split (~50 / 25 / 25 between var, κ₃, κ₄) means
the variance channel is worth roughly twice the other two per unit of per-layer relative
accuracy. Key consequence for planning: a 3× reduction in the variance-channel relative error
buys about the same final MSE as eliminating κ₄ exactly, and it is far cheaper. That ordering
is the spine of this answer.
Q1 — The variance channel (worth −47 %)
1.1 What the problem really is
Next-layer pre-activation variance on row i:
var_pre,i = w_iᵀ C_post w_i ,   C_post = Cov(h),  h = relu(z).
var_pre,i = Σ_j w_ij² C_post,jj  +  Σ_{j≠k} w_ij w_ik C_post,jk.        (1)
Diagonal part is 0.001 u/layer (a W∘W times a vector). Everything expensive is the off-diagonal
sum. The chain's own C_post is accurate in the diagonal and in K22 (R² 0.9–1.0) but carries a
random per-entry error of rms 4.2e-5 (≈ 2 % of a typical entry) that its closure terms explain
only 3–8 % of. So the fix is a missing term in the off-diagonal post-ReLU covariance, not a
better estimator of a known term.
1.2 The missing term is the Edgeworth g³ contribution to Cov(h_j,h_k)
Work with the standardised pair. Put u = z_j/σ_j, v = z_k/σ_k, ρ = C_jk/(σ_jσ_k), and g_j
= κ₃jjj/σ_j³, g_k = κ₃kkk/σ_k³, plus the mixed g₃(jjk) = κ₃(j,j,k) terms. Write h =
σ·φ(u), φ(u)=relu(u) (after centring: φ = relu(u) − E relu(u)). Expand the joint density to
third order (Edgeworth) and integrate against φ(u)φ(v):
Cov(h_j,h_k) = σ_jσ_k [ ρ·A(ρ)                                   (Gaussian 
part, what the chain has)
                  + g_j·σ_j·A_j(ρ) + g_k·σ_k·A_k(ρ)                (the 
⟨3,1⟩ marginals — partly present)
                  + g₃(jjk)·B_{jjk}(ρ)/σ_j + g₃(jkk)·B_{jkk}(ρ)/σ_k  (the 
MISSING (2,1) mixed-κ₃ term)
                  + κ₄-terms·A₄(ρ) ]  + O(g²).                      (2)
The decisive structural fact: the missing term is quadratic in the pre-activation third-moment
content of the pair, and it is a local functional of a 2×2 block — it needs only (C_jj, C_kk,
C_jk, κ₃jjj, κ₃kkk, κ₃jjk, κ₃jkk), i.e. the pair's own bivariate cumulants. The chain's
closure program is a Wick/Edgeworth program on the bivariate cumulants of each pair, so this term
is inside the existing closure machinery — it is a dropped order, not a new object. That is why
it is cheap and why it is the right first move. Concretely, with φ_c = φ − Eφ and a Hermite
expansion φ_c(u) = Σ_n c_n He_n(u)/√(n!), the (2,1) term is
δC_jk = (1/2) g_j c_1 c_2·( κ₃jjk/σ_j )·(∂_ρ A) + (1/2) g_k c_1 c_2·( κ₃jkk/
σ_k )·(∂_ρ A) + (c_2²/2)·( κ₃jjk κ₃jkk )/(σ_jσ_k)
c_1 = E[u φ_c(u)] = 1/2, c_2 = E[He_2(u)φ_c(u)]/√2 = (1/2 − 1/(2π))/√2 ≈ 0.1248.

Route A in detail
(a) State. Keep the existing C, κ₃(i,i,j) slice, and add nothing new except the pre-activation 
g_j vector and the κ₃(i,j,k)-free estimate κ₃(i,i,j) already propagated. O(n²).
(b) FLOPs. The mixed term is a per-entry correction built from three n-vectors (g, κ₃jjj,
κ₃jjk broadcast along j,k): one outer-product-like pass over the off-diagonal, ≈ 2n² flops = 
0.002 u for the correction itself. But the correction must then be used on the screen rows: for the
~350 screened rows, w_iᵀ δC w_i with δC represented in the pair-functional form. If we
materialise δC (n² = 1.05e6 entries) and multiply, that is 350·2n ≈ 0.0007 u — negligible. The
real cost is keeping C_post itself, which you already do. So Route A costs ≈ +0.01–0.03 u/
layer, not +0.30. The +0.30 figure in the table is the cost if you rebuild the whole off-diagonal C
with the new term every layer via dense products; the pair-functional form avoids that.
(c) Falsifier on stored arrays. For each layer, compute per-entry δC_jk := C_post^true,jk −
[chain C_post,jk] on the stored teacher. Regress δC_jk on the predicted pair-functional 
δĈ_jk(A) with all 7 pair cumulants as controls. Pass condition: slope 1.0 ± 0.15 and R² ≥ 0.5
(vs the current 3–8 % explained), and the rms of δC − δĈ ≤ 1.5e-5 (i.e. ≤ ~35 % of 4.2e-5). Also
check the sign prediction and that the residual is uncorrelated with κ₃jjj κ₃kkk. Runtime:
pure numpy on one 1024×1024 layer slice ×16 ≈ minutes.
(d) End-to-end. Add Route A to the chain, rerun the 6e7-sample mean recursion, and report
final MSE. Expected band 1.45e-8 – 1.55e-8 (i.e. capture 0.533 → ~0.66–0.70 of the variance
channel's −47 %), at +≤0.05 u/layer → total ≤ 261 u, budget fraction essentially unchanged. If
the end-to-end gain is < 5 % of MSE for +0.03 u, Route A is dead and Route B (per-row pair
law) becomes the fallback.
Route B in detail (the "adjoint" variant)
(a) State. The chain's C_pre, κ₃(i,i,j), and a sparse neighbour list (top-k |ρ| per screened
row): 350×512 int32 indices ≈ 0.7 MB. O(kn).
(b) FLOPs. Per screened row: assemble the pair-law block for k neighbours (O(k) 2×2 blocks,
each needing 7 cumulants → O(k)), evaluate the closed-form A(ρ) and the δ-correction (O(k)),
then the weighted sum (O(k)). Total 350·k·c with c≈30 ≈ 5.4e6 flops = 0.0025 u/layer.
Compare a full dense diag(W C Wᵀ) = 2n³ = 2 u/layer. This is the 800× win.
(c) Falsifier. Compare w_iᵀC_post^true w_i (from teacher) vs the sparse w_iᵀĈ_post w_i on
the 350 screened rows. Pass: relative error ≤ 0.1 % on ≥ 90 % of screened rows, with the tail
(rows where the dropped pairs carry >0.1 % weight) explicitly listed. A useful diagnostic: plot 
Σ_{dropped}|w_ij w_ik C_jk| vs the truncation k; the curve should flatten by k ≈ 512.
(d) End-to-end. Identical to Route A but only on the screened rows; the unscreened rows keep
the chain value. Expect 1.5e-8 at ≤ 261 u. Important honest caveat: Route B only helps if the
dominant pairs are the high-|ρ| ones — if the 4.2e-5 error is spread uniformly across all pairs,
the neighbour truncation loses nothing in cost but also gains little in accuracy; the falsifier in (c)
distinguishes the two regimes.
Q2 — The κ₄ diagonal (worth −30 %)
2.1 The exact contraction and why the chain misses the pattern
For h = relu(z), Z⁽ˡ⁺¹⁾ = W h, the fourth cumulant is the standard contraction (with K4 =
κ₄(h), K22 = κ₄ spin-off = Cov⊗Cov term):
κ₄(z_i') = Σ_j W_ij⁴ K4_jjjj                                (4,0,0,0) — the 
"level" term
        + 3 Σ_{j,k} W_ij² W_ik² K22_jjkk                   (2,2)     — also 
sets the level
        + 4 Σ_{j,k} W_ij³ W_ik K4_jjjk                     (3,1)
        + 6 Σ_{j,k,m} W_ij² W_ik W_im K4_jjkm              (2,1,1)   ← 
PATTERN
        +   Σ_{j,k,m,r} W_ij W_ik W_im W_ir K4_jkmr        (1,1,1,1) ← 
PATTERN
The measured fact is: the first two give slope 1.00 (level right) but corr 0.24–0.59 with truth
(pattern wrong). The pattern lives in (3,1)+(2,1,1)+(1,1,1,1). The right way to see why is to ask
what the post-ReLU κ₄ is born from at each layer, since the transported-from-old-birth
contribution is only ~10 % deep.
2.2 The birth law: post-ReLU κ₄ at depth is born from pre-ReLU κ₃ ⊗ C
Apply the Wick/Edgeworth program to a quadruple (h_{j}, h_{k}, h_{m}, h_{r}) with a
joint pre-activation density expanded to the order needed. For a jointly Gaussian pre-
activation quadruple, h=relu(z) has κ₄ determined entirely by the pairwise correlations —
this is the "Mehler/star" law, κ₄(h_r,h_r,h_k,h_m) = β_r a_kr a_mr, which is right at layer 2
but not deep, because deep the pre-activation quadruple is not Gaussian: the pre-activation κ₃
feeds back. The leading non-Gaussian birth term is the κ₃ ⊗ C coupling: schematically, at each
layer
κ₄(h_j,h_k,h_m,h_r)  ←  Σ over 3 pairings of [ κ₃(z_a,z_b,z_c) × C(z_d, z_·) 
× ∂φ×∂φ tags ]
                     +  κ₄(z_j,z_k,z_m,z_r) × Π (∂φ 
tags) ]                                    (BIRTH)
+ (transported old births: the ~10 % tail the chain already has)
Because φ = relu is piecwise linear and its derivative is a step function, the ∂φ tags are 
indicator functions Φ_j = 1[z_j>0], whose covariance with the others is exactly the pair-
closure data the chain already computes. So the birth term is a functional of the pre-
activation κ₃ slice and the covariance — i.e. all the ingredients are already in V29, only the 
coupling was never formed. This is the structural explanation of your "the deep κ₄ is
regenerated at every ReLU".
2.3 Evaluating the contraction w-by-w without the full tensor
A full K4 ∈ R^{n⁴} is impossible (n⁴ = 1.1e12). The trick is the same as Q1: represent the post-
ReLU κ₄ in a low-rank / pair-functional form so that the contractions become matrix products
the chain already does.
Ansatz:  K4_post[j,k,m,r] ≈ Σ_{s=1..R} α_s · u_s[j] u_s[k] u_s[m] u_s[r]
                                 + skew-embedded pair part Σ_s 
(a_s⊗a_s⊗b_s⊗b_s + perms)          (RANK-R)

u_s)_i²                                  — 3 matvecs per s
(1,1,1,1)_i ≈ Σ_s α_s (W 
u_s)_i⁴                                               — reuse the same W u_s
(3,1)_i  ≈ 4 Σ_s α_s ((W∘W)u_s²)_i (W 
u_s)_i                                   — reuse
level terms: (4,0,0,0)_i = Σ_s α_s ((W∘W)u_s²)_i² ; (2,2)_i = 3 Σ_s α_s 
((W∘W)u_s²)_i²  (with K22 matching)
So the entire diagonal κ₄ becomes one pass of R rank-1 matvecs, each of which is 2n² flops
for W u_s plus n² for (W∘W)u_s².
(b) FLOPs. Per layer: 3Rn² flops = 3R/2 · 0.001 u = 0.0015R u. With R = 32 → 0.048 u/layer; with
R = 128 → 0.19 u/layer. Both are far below one dense n³ product, and the rank-32 version keeps
the (2,1,1)/(1,1,1,1) pattern. Note the diagonal extraction is the whole point — you never form
K4.
(a) State. R vectors of length n plus R scalars — O(Rn) = 3.3e4 floats = 0.13 MB at R = 32. This
is the cheapest state in the whole problem.
How to get the u_s: they are the leading eigenvectors of the birth operator, not of the
transported K4. Practically: (i) sample the birth law (BIRTH) at layer ℓ by a randomized
estimator E[κ₃⊗C⊗Φ-tags] using a Gaussian sketch of size R₀ = 64; (ii) power-iterate 2–3 times
under the layer map T = Wᵀ diag(Φ); (iii) take the top-R u_s. Cost of the sketch ≈ R₀·2n² =
0.06 u/layer, amortised / reused from the chain's own κ₃ slice.
(c) Falsifier on stored arrays. You have true κ₄(i,i,i,i), κ₄(i,i,j,j), κ₄(i,i,i,j) per layer.
Test in order:
Level test: regress true κ₄ diagonal on the (4,0,0,0)+(2,2)+(3,1) parts. Slope should be 1.00
(already passes).
Pattern test (the real one): regress true κ₄ diagonal on the rank-R (2,1,1)+(1,1,1,1)
prediction, after subtracting the level. Pass: corr ≥ 0.60 (vs chain's 0.24–0.59) at R = 32, and
≥ 0.70 at R = 128. Report the corr-vs-R curve — that single curve decides the rank.
Birth-law test: on layer 2, confirm the Mehler star law reproduces κ₄ (already known right).
Then at layers 6, 10, 14, test the (BIRTH) term against κ₄_post^true − [transported old
births]. Pass: R² ≥ 0.5, and the fraction explained grows with depth (the birth term
should dominate by layer 10).
Neuron-of-matter test: restrict all of the above to |μ/σ| < 1.5. Current rel. error there is
~20 %; pass = ≤ 10 % at R = 32, ≤ 7 % at R = 128. (This is the number that matters — the
global corr is a distractor.)
(d) End-to-end. Inject the rank-R κ₄ diagonal into the mean closure at every layer, rerun the
recursion. Oracle says κ₄-diagonal-exact buys 0.699 → 1.600e-8. Expect 1.70e-8 – 1.75e-8 at R =
32 (+0.05 u/layer) and 1.62e-8 – 1.67e-8 at R = 128 (+0.19 u/layer). If the first is not achieved,
the pattern rank is too low; if the second is not achieved, the birth operator's eigenvectors are
wrong (go back to falsifier 3).

Conclusion up front: MC cannot compete as a production estimator of δvar/δκ₃/δκ₄ at 10⁴–10⁵
samples. The only viable role is (i) as an oracle-lite to fit/validate the closure terms of Q1/Q2,
and (ii) as a control-variate residual estimator where the chain supplies the mean and the MC
supplies only the small correction.
3.2 Rao–Blackwellisation over the last weight layer — the good idea
The final layer's mean is μ⁽ᴸ⁾ = W^{(L)} E[h^{(L−1)}], and the score only needs 
E[h^{(L−1)}]. RB over the last weight layer means: condition on h^{(L−1)}, and the last
layer's contribution is analytic (it is just W E[h]). So no sampling is needed for the last layer at
all — the chain already does this. The non-trivial RB is over the penultimate ReLU: condition
on z^{(L−1)}'s bivariate law and integrate relu analytically. That is precisely the closure the
chain already implements. So RB adds nothing the chain lacks — it re-derives it. The honest
conclusion is that the chain is the RB estimator; the residual error is purely in the input
cumulants fed to it.
3.3 Control variate built from the Gaussian surrogate — does the 100× materialise?
Setup: X = δvar_i (the correction), Y = the same functional computed under the chain's 
Gaussian surrogate (all κ₃ = κ₄ = 0, same C). Then E[Y] is known exactly in closed form, and
the CV estimator is X ̂  = X − β(Y − E[Y]) with β = Cov(X,Y)/Var(Y). Variance reduction is 1/
(1−ρ²_{XY}). 100× needs ρ ≥ 0.995.
Is ρ ≥ 0.995 plausible? The correction δvar is driven by the pair's non-Gaussian content (κ₃⊗C),
which is orthogonal to the Gaussian part to leading order — so ρ is small, not near 1. Verdict:
the 100× CV does not exist for δvar itself. But there is a better formulation: 
Use the chain's full prediction as the control, and fight over the model-error only.
Define X_i = w_iᵀ(C_post^true − C_post^chain)w_i and the control Y_i =
w_iᵀ(Ĉ_post^A − C_post^chain)w_i where Ĉ^A is Route A 's new closure. Since 
C_post^true ≈ Ĉ^A if Route A is right, Var(X−Y) = Var(δC^A_error), which is the 
residual rms, ~1.5e-5 instead of 4.2e-5, i.e. a ~8× variance / ~2.8× sd reduction. That is a
real but modest win, and it costs sampling. Do it once as the Q1 falsifier, not in
production.
3.4 Antithetic / stratified / QMC on the sphere
The network is 1-homogeneous: f(x)=|x|f(x/|x|), so the direction is the only randomness
that matters at scale. Directions live on S^{1023}, where a Gaussian design already achieves 
O(1/m) in expectation for smooth functionals but the ReLU activation gives an intrinsic 
m^{-1/2} barrier for the kink terms. Concretely:
Antithetic: x → −x is not useful — for ReLU, relu(−z) = 0, so the antithetic pair is strongly
negatively correlated in a way that changes the estimand. Instead use reflection in the
hyperplane orthogonal to a random direction — this preserves the mean. Expected
variance reduction: ≤ 2×. Not worth it.
• 
Stratification by |x|: since f = |x|·g(direction), sample the radial part semi-analytically
(chi-distribution) and stratify it. Reduction: 2–3×. Marginal.
QMC (Sobol on the sphere's tangent space): dimension 1023 is far too high; effective
dimension is low (the network's leading PCA directions), so a randomised-QMC with
PCA truncation to ~32 dims could give 3–10×. Still not 100×.
Realistic verdict for Q3: at 10⁴ samples (160 u) you get δvar to a relative accuracy of roughly 
σ_δ/√m · 1/δ. With δvar/var ≈ 0.15 \% and a per-sample variance giving an sd of ~2 % of
var, the sd of the mean correction at m = 10⁴ is 2e-2/100 = 2e-4 relative — that is worse than
the chain's 0.15 % = 1.5e-3 error? Actually comparable, so m = 10⁴ recovers roughly the same
accuracy as the chain at 160 u vs the chain's ~0.05 u for that channel. Dead as a production
route, useful as validation.
3.5 What MC is good for here
Sample 10³–10⁴ forwards once per layer (16–160 u, one-off), and use them to: (i) fit the
pair-functional coefficients B_{jjk}(ρ) in (2) directly — this is the fastest way to get Route
A right; (ii) measure the rank-R decay curve for κ₄ (Q2) — the spectrum tells you R; (iii)
provide the ground truth for the four falsifiers above. This is the highest-value use of
the sampling budget in the whole round. It is not an estimator; it is calibration data, and
it converts three guessy problems into three measured ones.
