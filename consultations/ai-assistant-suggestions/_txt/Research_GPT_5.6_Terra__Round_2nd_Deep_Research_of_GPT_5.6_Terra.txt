Mechanistic Estimation of Random Neural Networks
Second-round research report: aggregate response states and alternatives
Executive Summary
The strongest surviving direction is an aggregate response state, but the naive formulation
T_old = sum_a sym3(G_a(x)v_a) is not economically viable if G_a is transported as a dense
n x n matrix: its transport costs about 2 dense matrix multiplications per component per
layer, so k=100 already costs roughly 200 units per layer. The measured M1-M3 evidence
suggests that the required k is likely in the low hundreds, which would exceed the 1024-unit
budget over 16 layers. Therefore the useful version must either (i) exploit additional structure
in G, (ii) fit only the query-relevant state and refresh it selectively, or (iii) discover a
different global quenched representation.
The most informative immediate experiment is a one-layer age-gate fit of the aggregate
response state against the complete D21/D3 query family. If k below about 16-32 cannot reach
2% relative RMS, the dense-component route is effectively closed. If k around 16-32 succeeds,
a selective-refresh or structured-transport variant becomes worth pursuing.
1. Q1 — Concrete fitting procedure
Let the age-gate source content be represented by
W = (A n P) diag(w2), so that the relevant D21 query is U = W A^T.
Use a query-weighted low-rank approximation rather than an unweighted approximation of W.
Step 1 (derived): Build a validation matrix of all four sibling query operators used by the
ReLU closure: the D21 contribution, its transpose/cross arrangement, and the corresponding
D3/diagonal contractions. Normalize each by its measured RMS scale.
Step 2 (derived): Solve
min_{G_a,v_a} || Q({G_a,v_a}) - Q_exact ||_F^2 / ||Q_exact||_F^2
subject to a fixed component count k,
where Q concatenates the four query families. Initialize with the rank-k SVD of W A^T
(or W when A is well conditioned), then alternate least-squares updates for v_a and G_a.
The fit is performed once at the age gate, not independently for every later layer.
Step 3 (conjecture): Do not fit the full tensor. Fit only the D21/D3 observables because
those are the actual ReLU inputs. This avoids paying for unqueried all-distinct entries.
A useful diagnostic sequence is k = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128. Record the maximum relative
RMS over all four query families, not only D21. The target is <=2%.
Cost (derived): Forming W and the query matrices is O(n^2). A Lanczos/truncated-SVD-style
fit is roughly O(n^2 k), about 0.001 k units at n=1024 before implementation constants.
The expensive part is transport: if G is dense, G <- B G B^T costs about 2 units/component/layer,
and v <- Bv is small by comparison. Thus approximately 2k units/layer, before fitting and
closure overhead.
Expected k (measure-this): M1 says the raw Hadamard products are nearly full rank, while M3
says the exact contracted query has an effective rank around 230 at the 2% level. That makes
k»100-300 the plausible range for a static dense representation, which is far too expensive
to transport for 16 layers. The experiment is therefore primarily a falsifier: if k<=32 works,
there is a new low-dimensional phenomenon worth exploiting; if not, the straightforward
aggregate-state hypothesis should be abandoned.
CPU falsifier (measure-this): On one stored .npz state at the age-4 gate, construct W, fit
k=4..64, reconstruct all four query families, and compare against the exact source aggregate.
A 1024x1024 SVD plus several matrix products is comfortably a sub-hour CPU experiment.
2. A more economical variant: query-space response
packets
Conjecture: A better descendant of the aggregate-state idea is to store the action of the
deep tensor on a fixed packet of probe vectors rather than G itself.
Choose m probe vectors r_q (for example Gaussian or deterministic Hadamard probes) and store
y_q = kappa3 contracted with r_q in each required mode. Transport the probes forward/backward
with the same linear map and periodically reconstruct only the D21/D3 queries.
The state size is O(nm), not O(kn^2). Its weakness is exactly the M3 result: naive random
sketching had very poor variance. The new experiment should therefore use probes optimized
against the measured query covariance, not iid TensorSketch features. Fit the probes by
PCA/SVD of the empirical query-response covariance at the age gate.
Cost (conjecture): O(m n^2) for probe transport, approximately m/1024 dense-matmul units
per layer if implemented as an n x m matrix multiplication. For m=32, this is about 0.031
unit/layer for one mode, plus reconstruction/closure. The risk is that the required m may
again approach hundreds because of the flat odd signal spectrum.
3. Q2 — Other non-source formulations
Characteristic/cumulant-generating functional in a low-dimensional random-feature basis:
conjecture: unlikely under the current evidence. Parity forces the relevant realized odd
component to survive conditioning on even statistics, so a basis carrying only ensemble
moments cannot recover it. A random-feature implementation would inherit the observed
1/sqrt(d)-type sketching problem. First falsifier: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 feature dimensions
at one age gate; require <=2% D21 RMS.
Quenched random-tensor model with covariance plus a small realized odd basis:
derived obstruction: covariance is even and cannot determine the realized odd tensor because
of the exact parity result. The only potentially useful part is a compact realized residual.
That reduces to the aggregate-response problem. Therefore it is not a distinct escape unless
the residual is shown to have low intrinsic dimension.
Response/Jacobian statistics:
conjecture: plausible only if a response observable can be propagated without explicitly
shipping the third-order tensor. A candidate is a matrix of directional derivatives of the
ReLU expectation with respect to a small probe family. First falsifier: compare a 16- or
32-probe response basis with exact D21/D3 at the age gate. Failure above 2% closes this route.
Tensor-network contraction with birth index first:
derived: reordering contractions does not change the arithmetic barrier for the Hadamard
query. Contracting j first gives an n x n result whose entries each aggregate n birth
columns. Unless the aggregate has a new algebraic structure, this is the same O(n^3)
operation that M1-M3 expose. No credible cost reduction follows from ordering alone.
TAP/cavity:
conjecture: not currently credible at this operating point. The desired object is a
quenched, realized odd response, whereas conventional cavity/mean-field closures primarily
compress ensemble statistics. A useful TAP-like method would need an explicit finite-n ReLU
response equation and an O(n^2) update. No such closed equation is established in the
provided evidence. First falsifier: derive and implement a one-layer cavity update and
compare all D21 entries; >2% error closes it.
Feynman/diagrammatic resummation:
conjecture: potentially interesting theoretically, but not yet a computational escape.
The known cumulant hierarchy organizes the expansion, but the provided measurements give no
evidence that the realized odd diagrams collapse to a small sufficient statistic. The first
test should be whether the leading 2-3 diagram families reproduce the measured D21 residual
with <=2% RMS at one layer. If not, stop.
4. Q3 — What the leaders are most likely doing
Measure-this / conjecture: the strongest evidence is not that leaders simply dropped all
old sources. The closed sensitivity experiment explicitly says that removing old content
produces large errors and that every layer from roughly 8 onward matters. Therefore a pure
old-source truncation is inconsistent with the supplied measurements.
A more plausible single hypothesis is selective aggregation/compression of the old odd
response: the leaders preserve the realized deep signal but represent it in a cheaper
global state, while retaining the young-source tier. The cost coincidence (roughly 250-107
units) then reflects removal of the expensive source-by-source implementation, not removal
of the underlying information.
Confirming observable: take a leader-equivalent budget implementation and measure the
conditional intrinsic dimension of the aggregate old D21 response after whitening by the
even covariance/transport statistics. Compute q_90, q_95, q_98 and q_99 singular dimensions
for layers 8-15. If q_98 is O(10-50), a global compressed old-response state is strongly
supported. If q_98 is O(200-400), the leader must be exploiting a more specialized algebraic
or selective computation. If q_98 is near n, then the cost gap likely comes from a mechanism
not represented by the proposed aggregate state.
5. Bottom line
Derived from the closed measurements: there is no remaining justification for another
ordinary low-rank factorization of individual old legs, a covariance-only closure, a parity
mean-field approximation, or a tensor-sketch surrogate. Those mechanisms are directly
constrained by the measured flat Hadamard spectra and exact parity.
Conjecture: the remaining credible class is a global quenched response representation whose
state is optimized for the ReLU query family rather than for the full third-order tensor.
The most promising concrete implementation is a query-space response packet with
data-dependent probes, combined with occasional exact refresh at selected layers.
Measure-this: the single most valuable next CPU experiment is the age-gate intrinsic
dimension test using the exact stored old-source aggregate. It directly answers whether the
information itself is low-dimensional, independent of how the current V29 implementation
stores it. That measurement can decisively separate 'representation problem' from
'irreducible arithmetic problem' before another large implementation is attempted.
