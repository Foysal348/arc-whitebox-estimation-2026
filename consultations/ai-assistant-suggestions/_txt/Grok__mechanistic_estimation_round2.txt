Expected k from M1–M3 (C)
M3 states that the exact contracted object U is compressible to rank ≈ 230 for 2 % energy. Because each (G,v) pair
contributes a rank-at-most-n factor of a very special form, the effective degrees of freedom per component are only
n(n+1)/2 + n ≈ n²/2. Empirical CP rank of a generic sym3 tensor that matches a rank-r matrix query is typically
1.5–2 r. Hence the conjecture k ≈ 300–450. At k = 350 the per-layer transport cost is 700 units — already larger
than the entire budget B. Even an optimistic k = 80 (if the query rank of M3 overestimates the true structural rank)
still costs 160 units per layer, i.e. > B after only a few layers. Thus the aggregate-response idea, while
mathematically elegant, is almost certainly too expensive unless a dramatic further compression of the components
themselves is discovered.
First falsifier (one layer, one network, stored legs as .npz)
Load the dense legs of all sources of age exactly 4 at layer 6 (or 7). Form the four exact U matrices. Run the ALS
procedure above for k = 50, 100, 200, 300. Record relative Frobenius residual on U and the resulting relative RMS
error on D21. If residual > 2 % already at k = 200 the method cannot meet the error law at affordable cost; if it
succeeds only for k ≥ 250 the transport cost exceeds B. Runtime on a single CPU core with BLAS: < 20 min for the
whole sweep.
Q2. Formulations that are not a sum over sources
Every proposal below is required to stay outside the closed list (no per-source dense legs after age 4, no mean-field

(A) Quenched two-point statistics of κ₃ plus a small odd residual basis (C)
Page 1
Because κ₃ is odd under sign flips of W, its expectation vanishes, but its second moment E[κ₃ ⊗ κ₃] is even and, by
rotational invariance and the known Gaussian measure on the weights, is a linear combination of products of the
current covariance C. Concretely the only isotropic tensors of the correct symmetry are built from C⊗C⊗C with a
handful of distinct index contractions. One can therefore carry, for free, the exact second-moment structure of the
deep third cumulant. The realized (quenched) odd part that actually contributes to D21 is then represented by a
single low-rank factorisation of size n × r_odd with r_odd ≪ n (the “realized odd residual”). Update: the
second-moment tensors are functions of C alone and cost nothing extra; the residual factor is transported by the
same B = W diag(w₁) at cost 2 n² r_odd ≈ 0.4 units for r_odd = 64. Slices are read off by contracting the residual
against itself with the known second-moment kernel. Loss: any higher-order cumulants of κ₃ itself. Falsifier: at layer
10 compute the exact deep κ₃ contribution to D21; form the residual after subtracting the best rank-64
approximation consistent with the second-moment kernel; measure relative error. If > 3 % the residual rank is
insufficient.
(B) Tensor-network contraction that eliminates the birth index first (D/C)
A single source’s contribution is κ₃_ijk = Σ_m A_im A_jm P_km w₂_m (plus permutations). The birth index m can be
contracted before any later layer is applied, producing three n×n matrices already at birth: S_AA = A diag(w₂) Aᵀ,
S_AP = A diag(w₂) Pᵀ, S_PP = P diag(w₂) Pᵀ. Thereafter only these three matrices need to be transported: S ← B S Bᵀ.
Cost per source after birth: 6 units per layer (three independent covariance-style transports). For the whole old tier
this is still 60+ units — better than 107 but not yet at the leaders’ 0. Aggregation across sources is free (just sum
the S’s). The method is exact for every individual source; the only question is whether the summed S’s remain
full-rank or can themselves be low-rank compressed. Falsifier: form the summed S_AA, S_AP, S_PP of all sources
older than age 4 at layer 8; compute their singular spectra. If 99 % energy already requires > 400 directions the
subsequent transport stays expensive.
(C) Response-function / Jacobian statistics (C)
The final mean E[h^(L)] is a functional of the initial activation and of every intermediate weight. Its variation with
respect to a deep-layer pre-activation can be written as a product of Jacobians of the subsequent ReLU maps. The
third-order response (the object that multiplies κ₃) is exactly the third derivative of that composition. Because the
composition is a chain of element-wise nonlinearities interleaved with linear maps, the third response tensor admits
a recursive update that only needs the current first- and second-order response tensors (size n and n×n) and the
diagonal Hermite coefficients. Cost: a few dense n×n multiplies per layer (≈ 3–4 units total for the whole deep
response). The method never materialises κ₃; it only ever multiplies the arriving D21/D3 slices by the
already-computed response. Falsifier: implement the third-order response recursion on one network, inject the
exact deep D21 at a single intermediate layer, and verify that the final-mean perturbation matches the
finite-difference sensitivity map of M2 to relative 1 %. Any discrepancy kills the recursion.
Characteristic-function / random-feature expansions of the full layer map were considered and rejected: a
low-dimensional feature basis cannot capture the flat, noise-like spectrum of the Hadamard products (M1) while
remaining cheaper than forming those products. Spin-glass TAP / cavity expansions that keep only diagonal or
mean-field odd moments are forbidden by T1 (the conditional expectation vanishes). Feynman-diagram
resummations that stay at O(n²) recover at best the covariance + κ₄ regeneration already present in V29 and
cannot see the cross-neuron third cumulant.
Q3. Most likely explanation of the leaders’ cost/accuracy
Hypothesis (C, ranked first): the leaders keep the young-source tier exactly as V29 does (≈ 140–155 units) and
replace the entire old-source tier by a single dense n×n “effective D21 accumulator” that is updated by the exact
linear transport B D21 Bᵀ plus a cheap, parity-respecting closure for the off-slice remainder R. The closure uses only
the even algebra generated by C (the three monomials already written in the previous round) together with one or
two additional even tensors that can be maintained at O(n²) cost (e.g. the diagonal of C³ or a single learned scalar
field of size n). Because the accumulator is only one matrix, its transport costs ≈ 1.4 units per layer; over the 11–12
layers that contain old content the extra bill is ≈ 15–20 units, bringing the total into the observed 133–165 window.
The accuracy gain relative to pure young-source truncation (raw MSE ≈ 1e-6) comes from the fact that every layer
still receives a nonzero, correctly transported odd contribution, satisfying the sensitivity map M2.
Confirming observable (local experiment, < 30 min): replace V29’s old tier by a single D21 matrix transported
exactly and closed with the three-monomial formula for R; measure final MSE. If the result lands inside 1.6–2.5×10⁻⁸
the hypothesis is confirmed. If the error is still > 5×10⁻⁸, add the next even tensor suggested by a residual analysis
of R and repeat. The same experiment also falsifies any claim that a more exotic object (characteristic function, full
TAP expansion, etc.) is required.
Strongest argument that nothing cheaper remains: every scheme that retains a faithful representation of the deep
odd third cumulant must either (i) transport objects whose size is Ω(n²) (the slices themselves or equivalent
response tensors) or (ii) keep a factorisation whose rank is forced by M3 to be several hundred. Both routes cost at
least a few units per layer. The leaders’ margin over the pure young tier is only 0–25 units for the whole network;
that margin is precisely the cost of transporting one or two dense n×n matrices. Any proposal that claims zero
marginal cost for the deep content contradicts either parity (T1), the measured spectra (M1), or the sensitivity map
(M2).

Summary: the aggregate-response state of Q1 is elegant but almost certainly too expensive (k ≳ 200). The cheapest credible route
consistent with all closed measurements is a single dense D21 accumulator plus an even closure for the off-slice remainder (Q3). That is also
the most likely explanation of the leaders.
