Derived - shared squared propagators. For a row z of a shared left factor and a column x of a birth

Sharing only z∘z loses the cross terms. An exact symmetric-pair lift has d=n(n+1)/2=524,800
coordinates. One n×d float32 array occupies 2.002 GiB; multiplying it by a d×n array costs
n²(2d−1)/231=512.500 u per readout. With inner rank 384, d=73,920 and the corresponding product
still costs 72.187 u. These are direct lift costs, not universal lower bounds.
Derived - Gaussian column chaos. Conditional on prior-layer quantities and fixed gains, with
standard Gaussian row z, Q=diag(w1)X and σ²=2/n,
This is exactly degree-two chaos; it is not a small coefficient state. Multiplication by the third
transported leg produces cubic information. Keeping the realized chaos preserves oddness, but its
coefficients return the same mixed-index core. Your trace/Hermite/sketch failures already reject the
tested cheap truncations. A basis expansion alone supplies no additional saving.
Derived using your favorable pooled-core ledger. At r=250, the sum of slice evaluation, one birth

before other chain work. This exceeds 8 u/layer even under the stated optimistic counting. It does not

Already falsified - backward response. The source/query roles exchange, but mixed-index content
survives in the adjoint. Your adjoint measurement closes that implementation. I found no public
contraction schedule demonstrating a smaller complete bill on these tensors.
Assessment - kinetic or turbulence closures. A covariance of a quenched random tensor describes
uncertainty about its realized odd part; it does not identify that part. Exact projected dynamics instead
retain memory of unresolved components. A finite-memory closure would need new predictive evidence
beyond your slice-regression and pooled-state failures. I found none at the required accuracy and cost.
The 2.05-u figure is an implementation cost, not a proved arithmetic lower bound. The search
therefore supports “no demonstrated shortcut,” not “every possible shortcut is impossible.”

What the GitHub search added
Search scope. The exploratory sweep submitted 200 web queries, followed by targeted verification.
Nine retained native GitHub repository searches returned 56 distinct repository candidates. Those
counts are not counts of implementations read or validated, and do not constitute an exhaustive search
of GitHub. Appendix C provides a reproducible selection of queries.
Code-verified [S3–S6]. TensorLy's memory-saving MTTKRP iterates over rank components; pyttb's
sumtensor iterates over constituent tensors; opt_einsum caches identical intermediates. These help
storage or scheduling, but none gives a source-independent state for this readout. ARC itself includes a
symbolic whole-network contraction implementation; its documentation identifies it as a deprecated
expansion and warns of impractical constants. This is relevant existing infrastructure, not a certified
133–165-u competitor.
Code-verified [S7]. AlphaTensor's released real-arithmetic 4×4 factorization has shape (3,16,49): 49
scalar multiplications, equal to two Strassen levels. This specific release entry does not halve the bill.
Modulo-two records cannot simply be applied to float32 tensors. I am not claiming a lower bound on
other real bilinear algorithms.
Potentially relevant, not a proposed solution [S8]. The 2026 AlternatingCrossInterpolation
implementation avoids explicitly expanding TT Hadamard ranks to χ² when the output is compressible.
Its state is TT cores; local updates contract interpolation frames and refactor a two-site block. It still
requires a small output bond dimension. Your pooled three-mode TT result rejects that partition. A
different binary/quantics partition was not measured, but no cheap, query-accurate transport under
dense random W has been established. Pricing only the Hadamard sweep would omit the hard part.
Other nearby repositories concern low-rank covariance/Riccati projection, symmetric contractions of
materialized tensors, marginal skewness, or Phase 1 sampling at 256×32. They do not supply the
missing 1024×16 quenched cross-cumulant mechanism. Appendix A distinguishes inspected code from
documentation-only leads.
What this says about the leaders
Unknown. I did not find a disclosed leading Phase 2 implementation that explains the gap. The
numerical match between their bill and the young-tier bill does not prove what they carry. A 1.3×
raw-accuracy margin likewise does not prove an additional cumulant order. V29 already has a cheap K4
regeneration channel [S2]. There is no verified new K4 implementation to price here.
I cannot name a demonstrated approximation that halves the complete bill while losing <1.3× in MSE.
Recommending source deletion, hub screening or closure retuning would contradict your
measurements.

FLOPs. Isolating AAP adds two n²(2n−1) products per selected source, less than 2.01 u including

First falsifier. Any complete-query error above 2% at a checkpoint rejects treating the MPP-only object
as the full source for that network. If it passes, that is merely permission to test a complete recomputed
chain, not proof of final-mean accuracy. The executed 92.33% structural test makes success unlikely,
but does not replace this network-specific measurement.
Why this matters. It prevents spending another round optimizing a tensor that the estimator does not
actually propagate. It also makes a proposed 1.2-u algorithm reviewable: its timed operation list must
reproduce both AAP and MPP, thin corrections, joins and basis changes.

unvalidated for the actual chain. The useful product of this search is the corrected algebra and
reproducible falsifier. A budget-breaking representation, and the leaders' mechanism, remain
unresolved.

Appendix B / Reproduce the width-1024 diagnostic
The RNG precheck consumption is retained so these numbers match the executed audit. All matrix work is float32; float64 is
used only for diagnostic norms. Formula construction and readout cost <9.1 u including the parity check; random-number
generation is not part of the proposed estimator.
# NumPy only. Structural diagnostic, not a final-mean benchmark.
import os
os.environ["OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS"] = "1"
os.environ["OMP_NUM_THREADS"] = "1"
os.environ["MKL_NUM_THREADS"] = "1"
import numpy as np
rg = np.random.default_rng(20260921)
# Match RNG consumption of the small exact-tensor precheck.
rg.normal(size=(8, 8)); rg.normal(size=8)
rg.normal(size=(8, 8))
n = 1024
s = np.float32(np.sqrt(2/n))
W0 = rg.standard_normal((n,n), dtype=np.float32) * s
W1 = rg.standard_normal((n,n), dtype=np.float32) * s
C = W0 @ W0.T
w2 = np.float32(1/np.sqrt(2*np.pi)) / np.sqrt(np.diag(C))
A0 = np.float32(.5) * C
np.fill_diagonal(A0, 0)
# Exact repeated-index slice of the path tensor at birth.
birth = (A0*A0) * w2[None, :]
M0 = np.float32(3) * birth.T
P = np.float32(.5) * W1
A = P @ A0
M = P @ M0
def path(A, P):
    return (np.float32(2)*((A*P*w2[None,:]) @ A.T)
            + (A*A*w2[None,:]) @ P.T)
full = path(A, P)
rep = ((P*P) @ M.T + np.float32(2)*(P*M) @ P.T)/3
def norm(X):
    return np.sqrt(np.sum(X.astype(np.float64)**2))
print(norm(full-rep)/norm(full))       # 0.92329569
print(norm(np.diag(full-rep))/
      norm(np.diag(full)))            # 0.80810083
print(norm(path(-A,-P)+full)/norm(full))  # 0
Independent n=8 validation also materialized the two order-three tensors, transported all three axes, and compared their
exact slices with these formulas. Agreement was within 2.56×10⁻¹³ in float64. This verifies the algebra without claiming
full-network accuracy.
