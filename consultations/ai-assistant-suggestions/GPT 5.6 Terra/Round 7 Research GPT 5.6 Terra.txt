Round 7 – Computing the Three Per-Neuron Corrections Cheaply
Research analysis
Executive conclusion
The supplied measurements say the remaining error is concentrated in only three marginal quantities per
neuron: variance, diagonalκ3, and diagonalκ4. The channel oracle gives final-MSE ratios0.533, 0.858,
and 0.699 when each channel is made exact, so variance is the largest measured opportunity.
The strongest cheap result is an exact identity for all requested target variances: ifW = [w1,...,w n] and
C is the post-ReLU covariance, then
v = diag(W⊤CW )
can be evaluated exactly with one densen×n byn×n product, CW, followed byn dot products. At
n = 1024 this is about1.001 uper layer. It is not cheaper than the existing full-covariance propagation
by itself, but it is a clean oracle and a useful implementation baseline.
For further savings, the most defensible experiment is not top-k weight truncation (cross terms make
that approximation structurally unsafe), but atarget-neuron selective correction: preserve the chain’s
variance and estimate only the residual on the 300–400 important neurons using stored exactC and a
low-rank/diagonal decomposition of theerror covariance. Its feasibility can be decided directly from one
stored layer.
Forκ4, the previous deep-research draft contained a dangerous shortcut: the(2, 1, 1) fourth-cumulant
term is a fourth-order object, not a quadratic form of the third-cumulant slice. The correct contraction
requires aK4(j,j, :, :) family. The useful new route is therefore to approximate thebirth contributionto
these K4(j,j, :, :) slices from the local ReLU closure, then contract the resulting structured terms with
each target rowwi without storing the full four-tensor.

Q2.2 New cheap route: local-birth factorization
Conjecture. The measured fact that the deepK4 diagonal is regenerated at every ReLU suggests treating
the new (2, 1, 1) content as a local birth term. Under a second-order multivariate Edgeworth/ReLU
closure, the leading source is a sum of products of local derivatives with the pre-activationκ3 and
covariance:
Mj≈αj diag(aj)C diag(bj) +βj (C◦Aj) +γjRj,
where Aj is a vector derived from the storedK3(j,j, :) slice, and the scalar coefficientsαj,β j,γ j are
exactly the ReLU/Hermite coefficients already used by the chain. The important property is separability:
each birth term is a sum of rank-1 or Hadamard-times-covariance pieces, so its contraction withwi
reduces to dot products involving

without materializing a four-tensor.

End-to-end acceptance criteria
1. Variance: target-neuron relative error≤ 0.1% and a measurable final-MSE reduction toward the
1.22× 10−8 oracle.
2. κ4: relative error≤ 10% on the closure-relevant neurons, with a final-MSE reduction toward the
1.60× 10−8 oracle.
3. Production budget: any combined method should remain well below the 1024-unit hard budget, and
preferably close to the leaders’ 133–165-unit range.
Bottom line
The strongest new fact is that the variance problem can be madeexact for the important 300–400 neurons
at only about0.34 uof matrix-multiply work per layer onceCpost exists. The remaining research question
is whether the covarianceerror itself has a low-dimensional image under these target quadratic forms.
That is a falsifiable stored-array question.
Forκ4, the correct target is not a third-cumulant quadratic form. The viable route is a local-birth
factorization of the(2, 1, 1) and, if necessary, residual(1, 1, 1, 1) sectors. Its success or failure can be
determined from the already-stored slice arrays before any expensive production implementation.
