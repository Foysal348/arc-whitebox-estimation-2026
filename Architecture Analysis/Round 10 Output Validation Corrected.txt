2. Specific errors in the uploaded answer
 Row-stacked transport is mispriced.
For Z=[P;A] in R^(2n x n), the written block operator (I2 kron M) is 2n x 2n. A generic multiply costs 2*(2n)*(2n)*n = 8n^3 FLOPs
= 4 u. Exploiting the block-diagonal structure reduces it to two independent n x n products = 2 u, not 1 u. Therefore stacking P
and A does not halve transport.
Four-age batching has the wrong real shape.
For one D21 term, four age blocks concatenate as n x 4n multiplied by 4n x n. That costs 2*n*(4n)*n = 8n^3 FLOPs = 4 u,
exactly the cost of four separate square products. There are two D21 terms, so batching is FLOP-neutral.
The 6.5 effective layers normalization is circular.
It is not a structural count. The exact combinatorics are 50 D21 source-target pairs through L15 and 54 young source-target
state uses through L16. Using 104/16=6.5 and then multiplying per-layer costs by 6.5 creates the contradictory 52 u / 104 u
passages in the uploaded answer.
The shared-suffix saving is not established.
Precomputing a consecutive-map suffix requires a dense map product; applying that suffix to a source-specific birth matrix/leg
requires another dense product. The exact hierarchical schedule therefore pays 39 interval-map products + 54 birth applications
+ 100 contractions = 193 products - exactly the forward count.
The final 5.55 u/layer and 49-55 u claims are unsupported.
They depend on subtracting an unproven suffix reuse and on the incorrect stacked-transport arithmetic. They also conflict with
the answer's own final paragraph, which says the young tier remains about 86 u.
