Deterministic  Particle  Propagation  for  Next‑Layer
Cumulant Channels
Summary The proposed scheme is  Deterministic Nyström‑Tail Compression
(dNTC). For each of the  alive sources at a pre‑activation layer, it carries a landmark
cross‑matrix  with  fixed landmark coordinates, a diagonal
correction  vector  ,  a  small  core  tensor   for  third‑order
contributions, and a length‑  vector  for fourth‑order diagonals. No full 
dense legs or explicit  tensor are stored; the per‑source memory is  instead
of  . The per‑layer cost is dominated by updating the landmark matrices and
forming an approximate full covariance via a low‑rank plus diagonal representation,
consuming at most 15 square products (measured in  ‑equivalent multiply‑adds),
which  respects  the   u  budget.  The  one  falsifier  is  a  free‑run  channel‑wise
error gate: propagate the dNTC state from layer 1, extract per‑neuron variance, 
,   diagonal, and off‑diagonal   at layers 7/11/14/15, compare them
against the 60M‑sample teacher arrays using relative errors, and kill the scheme if
any channel error exceeds  the chain’s reference error on that channel at two
or more deep layers or if the error growth with depth is steeper than the chain’s.

Each  source  (s)  is  characterised  by  a  dense  leg:  two  (n\times  n)  matrices,  (P^{(s)})  (the
source’s contribution to the pre‑activation covariance) and (A^{(s)}) (the linear map from the
previous layer’s post‑activation mean and variance to the source’s contribution). Both are of
full rank (n=1024) for a newly created source. Storing and operating on all sources densely would
require (O(k,n^2)\approx k\cdot 10^6) numbers, far beyond the available budget.
To limit cost, the chain compresses older sources according to an age schedule:
Ages 1–4 (recent sources): kept dense, (1024) directions each;
Ages 5–7: represented in a shared basis of rank (384);
Ages (\ge 8): represented in a nested sub‑basis of rank (224).
The  compression  is  applied  to  the  per‑source  matrices  (P^{(s)})  and  (A^{(s)})  individually,
lowering the number of stored parameters. The chain’s cost is near the budget ceiling of ≤ 125 u
(one “u” ≈ one (1024\times 1024) matrix product), translating to roughly 15 square products
per layer.
Why the Hadamard Products for (Y_{21}) Remain Full Rank
The matrix (Y_{21}) can be written as a sum over sources of terms that involve the Hadamard
(element‑wise)  product  of  (P^{(s)})  and  (A^{(s)}).  For  a  source  with  dense  legs  (P^{(s)})  and
(A^{(s)}), its contribution takes the form [ Y_{21}^{(s)} = \bigl(A^{(s)}\odot P^{(s)}\bigr),M^{(s)} ;+;
\text{lower‑order  corrections},  ]  where  (\odot)  denotes  the  Hadamard  product  and  (M^{(s)})
incorporates the source’s mean and the weight geometry. Crucially, the Hadamard product of

1.1 Motivation
The chain’s current mixed‑rank representation keeps per‑source dense   and   matrices
( , , full rank) for sources up to age 4, and compresses older sources into shared or
nested bases. This collapses at the off‑diagonal third cumulant , because the
elementwise  Hadamard  products  needed  for   are  not  low‑rank  in  the  compressed  bases.
Retaining all dense legs would cost  memory and  compute, which is infeasible as
the number   of alive sources grows with depth (from 6 at L7 to 14 at L15). Removing sources
older  than  10  layers  still  leaves  a  16%  relative  error  in   at  L15.  We  need  a  scheme  that
computes  the  next‑layer  means  and  all  required  higher‑order  channels—per‑neuron  variance,
diagonal skewness , diagonal kurtosis , and the off‑diagonal  entries—
without carrying the full  tensor or the per‑source dense legs.
1.2 Core Idea
Replace  each  alive  source  by  a  fixed  set  of   deterministic  particle  vectors in  .  The
particles  are  chosen  at  source  birth  so  that  the  empirical  ensemble  matches  the  source’s
marginal  moments  (mean,  variance,  skewness,  kurtosis)  up  to  fourth  order,  and  respects  the
isotropic  covariance  structure.  Under  affine  transformations  (weight  matrices)  the  particles
evolve exactly; elementwise ReLU is applied particle‑wise. The pre‑activation at layer  is the sum
of  contributions  from  all  sources,  giving  a  total  cloud  of   particles.  All  required
inter‑source  channels—mean,  covariance,  ,  ,  and  —are  extracted  directly  from  this
cloud as empirical cumulants. No   per‑source dense legs and no full   tensor are ever
stored or manipulated.

1.4 Deterministic Initialization of Particle Ensembles
At birth, a source is an isotropic random vector   with zero mean, covariance  ,
and  known  univariate  skewness   and  excess  kurtosis   (initially  Gaussian  for  depth‑gated
sources, later a pushforward through ReLU). We construct an ‑particle ensemble that satisfies
the following exactly:
The empirical covariance equals .
All third‑order mixed moments   vanish, so the off‑diagonal   at birth is exactly
zero.
For every coordinate direction, the empirical first four moments match the target marginal
distribution: mean , variance , skewness , kurtosis .
The construction uses a spherical ‑design. A spherical ‑design on  is a finite set of 
unit vectors   such that for every polynomial of degree  , the arithmetic mean over the
design  equals  the  integral  over  the  uniform  measure  on  the  sphere.  For  ,  designs  with
 (the cross‑polytope, i.e. the coordinate axes  ) exist for any  . Augmenting with
additional points from the orbit of a suitable reflection group gives a ‑design of size  as low
as  or similar; for  a ‑design with  can be obtained numerically
(e.g. by minimizing the spherical cap discrepancy). The key property is that empirical moments
of odd degree vanish, guaranteeing isotropy up to order .
We then assign two radial levels to the direction vectors. Let the directions be paired into
antipodal sets. For each antipodal pair , we choose two radii  and weights  (with
) such that the average of  and  over the mixture matches  and the desired
fourth moment . Solving
yields a unique non‑negative solution for  when  (we set  for
simplicity and adjust radii accordingly). Because the directional vectors come from a  ‑design,
the  composite  ensemble  with   particles  (two  per  direction)  reproduces  the  target
marginal distribution exactly in every projection and has isotropic covariance. Skewness is zero
by symmetry. This initialization introduces no systematic error at birth; the empirical cumulants
equal  the  true  cumulants  of  the  source  distribution  up  to  order  four  for  all  diagonal  and
off‑diagonal quantities.

2.2 Construction and Content of the Particle Set
At the output of the ReLU non‑linearity of layer , source  is represented by the matrix 
,  whose  columns   are  the  particles.  The  set  is  deterministic  and
moment‑matched: we select a quadrature rule or an extension of the unscented transform that
not  only  matches  the  source’s  mean  and  covariance  (as  the  standard  unscented  transform
does) but also aligns its marginal skewness and diagonal kurtosis to the target values prescribed
by the analytic propagator. The same point set is used to estimate cross‑source outer products for
,  the  uncentered  third‑moment  tensor  that  feeds  .  No  per‑neuron  basis  change,  age‑

2.3 Per‑Layer State Evolution
The state evolves through each layer via three simple steps:
Linear  transform: Every  particle  is  multiplied  by  the  weight  matrix  ,
yielding pre‑activation particles .
Element‑wise activation: The ReLU (or any subsequent activation) is applied individually:
.
Aggregation  of  summary  channels: From  the  post‑activation  particles  we  compute  the
quantities needed by the next layer’s analytic machinery. For each source we obtain the
per‑neuron  mean  ,  variance  ,  skewness  ,  and  diagonal  kurtosis

2.4 Cost Analysis in ‑Equivalent Units
A single matrix‑vector product costs   multiply‑add operations; propagating   particles
through  therefore consumes  floating‑point operations. Expressed in the natural unit
of one full  matrix multiplication (a “square product”), the equivalent number of square
products per layer is . With , , and ,
The aggregation step adds a second block of   work because   can be built by
accumulating  rank‑one  updates:  for  each  particle  we  form  the  outer  product
,  an   operation.  The  total  for  both  phases  therefore  costs  at  most
,  or   square  products  per  layer.  Additional   scalar  moment
calculations are negligible.
The chain’s allocated total budget is   u, where “u” is calibrated such that a full
forward pass using fifteen   square products per layer would still be permissible (i.e.,   u
corresponds roughly to  square products per layer across the network). The particle scheme
consumes less than half a square product per layer, leaving over 99 % of the budget untouched.
Memory is likewise minimal: the state consists of  floats, fitting comfortably in
on‑chip SRAM.
2.5 Seamless Integration into the Chain
Replacing  the  per‑source  dense  legs  and  mixed‑rank  bases  demands  no  architectural
change. Internally, the chain retains its teacher‑compatible interface: functions that return the
mean vector, covariance matrix,  tensor, and  diagonal keep identical signatures. The new
implementation  simply  populates  these  arrays  from  the  particle  moments.  The  downstream
analytic machinery – the same that computes next‑layer mean and variance under ReLU, or the
off‑diagonal  correction – continues to operate unchanged.
During Stage 1 verification, the scheme can be run either in “teacher‑force” mode (true
layer‑  inputs are used to compute layer‑  channels) or free‑running from layer 1 onward.
The  particle‑derived  channels  –  variance,  ,  ,  and  off‑diagonal   –  will  be  compared
against 60M‑sample teacher arrays at layers 3, 7, 11, 14, 15, 16. The acceptance gate is stringent: no
channel error may exceed the chain’s current error (e.g., 4.6–6.6 % for  off‑diagonal), and no
channel may be  worse at two or more deep layers. Stage 2 integrates the scheme end‑to‑
end and measures the final‑layer mean MSE against the ‑sample truth, targeting a combined
score chain. With  the particle approximation is expected to be a

3. Falsifier and Two‑Stage Evaluation Plan
3.1 Deterministic Nyström‑Tail Compression (dNTC)
The proposed scheme avoids carrying the full per‑source dense (P_s, A_s \in \mathbb{R}^{n
\times n}) (n = 1024) and the full third‑order tensor (\kappa_3). It replaces each old source with a
deterministic low‑rank‑plus‑diagonal approximation built from a shared set of (m=120) landmark
coordinates (\mathcal{J} \subset {1,\dots,n}). The state propagated per source is:
a landmark cross‑matrix (C_s^{\text{(land)}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}),
a diagonal correction vector (\delta_s \in \mathbb{R}^n),
a small core tensor (\mathcal{T}_s^{\text{(core)}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m \times m}) for
third‑order contributions,
a length‑(n) vector (\kappa_{s,4}^{\text{(diag)}}) for the diagonal of the fourth cumulant.
These objects are updated recursively using the previous layer’s activation statistics and
weight  structure,  without  ever  forming  full  (n  \times  n)  or  (n  \times  n  \times  n)  arrays.  The
approximate  covariance  is  assembled  as  [  \hat{C}  \approx  D  +  \sum_s  C_s^{\text{(land)}, T}
\bigl(C_s^{\text{(land)}}  C_s^{\text{(land)}, T}\bigr)^{\dagger}  C_s^{\text{(land)}},  ]  where  (D  =
\operatorname{diag}!\bigl(\sum_s  \delta_s\bigr)).  All  operations  reduce  to  matrix‑vector
products with the (m \times n) landmark matrices and inversion of small (m \times m) blocks.
With  (m=120)  the  per‑layer  update  costs  at  most  15  square  products  (measured  in  (n^2)
multiply‑add equivalents), well within the ≤ 125 u budget (≤ 15 square products per layer).
The single falsifier is a free‑run channel‑wise error gate: the dNTC state is propagated from
layer 1  onward,  the  per‑neuron  channels  (variance,  (d_3  =  \kappa_3(z_i,z_i,z_i)),  (\kappa_4)
diagonal,  off‑diagonal  (C),  and  D21)  are  extracted  at  layers  7,  11,  14,  and  15,  and  compared
against the teacher truth arrays.
3.2 Stage 1 — Channel‑Error Gate on Stored Arrays
We run dNTC in free‑running mode, without access to intermediate teacher statistics, using
only the true input distribution. At each gate layer the following channels are compared with the
60M‑sample Monte‑Carlo teacher arrays (pre‑activation and post‑ReLU at layers 3/7/11/14/15):
per‑neuron  variance  ((\operatorname{diag}(C))),  split  into  diagonal  relative  error  and
off‑diagonal Frobenius norm error via D21;
diagonal third central moment (d_3);
diagonal fourth cumulant (\kappa_4);
off‑diagonal covariance D21 relative Frobenius error (|\text{off}(C)|_F / |C|_F).
All errors are relative and computed per layer. The chain’s own errors on these channels are
measured on the same teacher arrays as baseline; the known chain D21 errors are:

3.3 Stage 2 — Final‑Layer Mean MSE Integration (Decisive)
The dNTC state is integrated into the full chain: sources aged 1–4 are kept in the exact dense
representation,  while  sources  aged  ≥ 5  are  handled  by  the  dNTC  compressed  state.  The
evaluation runs on the same 100 MLP configurations (architecture, activation, initialisation) from
the competition dataset.
We  measure  the  final‑layer  (layer 16)  empirical  mean  squared  error  (MSE)  against  a
1e9‑sample  Monte‑Carlo  truth  for  each  MLP ,  paired  with  the  baseline  chain’s  MSE  from  an
identical run. The aggregated score is [ \text{Score} = \text{MSE}_{\text{dNTC}} \times \text{cost}, ]
where cost is in units “u” (1 u ≈ one (n^2) multiply‑add). The top‑3 viability thresholds are:
Score ≤ 0.44 × chain, achievable via: 
MSE ≤ 1.07 × chain at cost ≤ 102 u, or
MSE ≤ 0.95 × chain at cost ≤ 115 u, or
MSE ≤ 0.84 × chain at cost ≤ 125 u.
A prototype that does not meet the top‑3 threshold but stays within Score ≤ 0.8 × chain
qualifies as a  plausible path and is kept for further development. In this case the report must
name the specific missing term (e.g., “off‑diagonal (\kappa_3) contribution from sources aged
≥ 8”) that prevents closing the gap. Any scheme with Score > 0.8 × chain is considered dead.
The  relative  error  metric  is  the  normalised  L2  distance  (|  \mu_{\text{dNTC}}  ‑
\mu_{\text{true}} |^2 / | \mu_{\text{chain}} ‑ \mu_{\text{true}} |^2), averaged over the 100 models.
