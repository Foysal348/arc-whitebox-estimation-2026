Round 10 | Can the young tier be halved?
One mechanism, an exact cost obstruction, and a stored-leg test | 24 September 2026
Answer: no qualifying method established. I tested a different arithmetic representation: two-way numerical
packing in float64. It retains the full dense legs and realized signs. It can combine two products into one, but the
installed production meter charges float64 at twice float32. Thus this mechanism cannot deliver the requested 104
u to 55 u reduction. This is a no-go result for this packing substitution, not for every possible representation.
The one method assessed
Keep the original P and A for every young source; four ages occupy 8n2 float32 numbers, or 32 MiB. Form
quantized integer-valued operands in float64, plus O(n2) packing buffers. No source, hub, tensor entry, or sign is
averaged away. Numerical packing is an established approximate matrix-multiplication technique [1]; the following
application is the method assessed here.
For transport, quantize M, P and A with known scales, and calculate one square product Mq(Pq + B Aq). Decode its
low and high signed radix-B coefficients to obtain both transported legs, then undo their individual scales. Here M
is the actual transport map, including the Wick scaling when applicable.
For the requested slice, define four n-by-n factors (the division by 3 is included):
L1 = (P*P) diag(w)/3; R1 = A.T
L2 = 2(P*A) diag(w)/3; R2 = P.T
D21 = L1 R1 + L2 R2. (* means entrywise product.)
Choose quantization scales so both desired products have the same dequantization factor s. For the integer-valued
quantized factors, compute
For reproducibility, set Q = 511, c = max over j of max|Lj| max|Rj|,
aj = max|Lj|/Q, bj = c/(Q^2 aj), Ljq = round(Lj/aj),
Rjq = round(Rj/bj), and s = c/Q^2. Zero terms can be skipped.
Z = (B L1q + L2q)(B R2q + R1q)
= B^2 L1q R2q + B(L1q R1q + L2q R2q) + L2q R1q.
T = round(Z/B); Dhat = s [T - B round(T/B)].
In exact arithmetic this extracts the middle coefficient when the low coefficient and the signed middle coefficient lie
strictly between -B/2 and B/2. Floating-point error also needs sufficient guard space. The state can reproduce odd
fluctuations because it encodes the actual signed legs. Quantization and Strassen roundoff still require
validation; algebra alone does not guarantee 2% accuracy.
Exact cost obstruction
Let S be the charged cost of one float32 square product at the chosen Strassen depth. The same arithmetic in
float64 costs 2S. A pair of float32 products therefore costs 2S before substitution and 2S after substitution, before
packing, quantization and decoding. Those additional operations have nonnegative cost. Shared left-operand
preprocessing in the original transport does not create an advantage for the packed version.
Consequently, replacing every eligible two-product pair cannot halve its product bill. At the prompt's S
approximately 0.51 u, four ordinary products cost 2.04 u; two packed float64 products also cost 2.04 u, plus
preprocessing. Any unpaired newborn product remains chargeable.

The reference for these errors uses the original stored legs in float64. The test uses classical matmul on one
source, not Strassen, not the full fused production slice, and not a recurrent four-age rollout. Quantization accounts
for the observed error. Quantization, packing and decoding were excluded from the charged totals to give the

Conclusion: this concrete mechanism fails the cost gate even on a slice where its numerical error is below 2%. I
have no justified 55 u method and no general arithmetic lower bound ruling one out. This result establishes only
that two float32 products replaced by one float64 product cannot supply the required savings under this meter.
