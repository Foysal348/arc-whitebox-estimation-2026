Closure contribution per source:

Writing entries gives:
Term 1: [(P∘P)·diag(w)·Aᵀ]_(i,k) = Σ_j w_j a_j · P_ij² P_kj
Term 2: [(P∘A)·diag(w)·Pᵀ]_(i,k) = Σ_j w_j a_j · P_ij³ P_kj
Both terms share the trailing factor P_kj, hence the exact collapse:
D21_l^(b)_(i,k) = (1/3) Σ_j g_(i,j)^(b,l) · P_(b→l)_(k,j), g_(i,j) = w_j a_(b,j) · P_ij²(1 + 2P_ij)

(1,1), matching the collapsed form.

3. Best orders found
Order A — collapsed forward (recommended). Keep P_(b→l), advance P ← M·P, never form A, build G entrywise, then
compute G·Pᵀ. Exact cost: 108.2 u for 54 effective pairs.
Order B — forward ages 1–2, reverse ages 3–4. Reverse mode carries both query legs and still requires the same
dense contraction. Cost remains 2.003 u/pair; no saving.
Order C — precomputed consecutive maps. Precomputing Q_t = M_tM_(t−1) costs about 15 u. Some transport is
reduced, but ages 3–4 still need additional products and the Q_t are not reusable across all birth/target offsets. It
does not beat the collapsed order under the stated ledger.
Order D — rank/sharing check. G^(b,l) differs by birth-specific w_b, a_b and P_(b→l), so no exact common factor
exists across births. Dense full-rank contractions remain 2n³.

4. Verdict
No mixed or hierarchical contraction order beats the current cost by 10% under the stated exact dense model; the
≤55 u target is not attainable by reordering alone.
The per-pair lower-bound structure is one dense transport (1.000 u) + one dense contraction (1.000 u) + O(n²)
entrywise work. Over 54 effective pairs this is about 108 u.
Why the floor is pinned: distinct births have non-nested P; G is birth-dependent; batching is FLOP-equivalent; reverse
mode is an isomorphic contraction order.
Real saving already realized: never form A. Further exact gains are primarily memory-side (incremental
accumulation), not another contraction-order trick.
