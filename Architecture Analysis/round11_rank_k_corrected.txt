Round 11 — Change What is Represented
Corrected technical assessment of the aggregated rank-k old
third-cumulant proposal
Bottom line. The supplied aggregated rank-k idea is a stronger candidate than the earlier query-only
construction because it preserves long-lived old third-cumulant information in a transport-compatible
representation. However, the previous PDF overstated what follows from a rank-230 Frobenius
approximation and understated the cost/accounting and nonlinear recompression issues. The scheme
should be treated as a promising candidate, not as a demonstrated ≤125u solution.
1. State carried per layer
Carry the usual mean μ and covariance C, plus an aggregate old third-cumulant representation:
 T_old ≈ Σ(a=1..k) σ_a · u_a ⊗ u_a ⊗ v_a.
This removes per-source dense legs and never materializes the full n×n×n tensor. The representation
is transport-compatible under a linear map B because each factor can be pushed forward:
 u_a ← B u_a, v_a ← B v_a.
2. What is genuinely good about it
 • It attacks the right object. The old κ3 contribution is long-lived and coherent, while the final mean is
 mainly sensitive through next-layer mean shifts. An aggregate representation can preserve that
memory without retaining every source separately.
• Transport is exact before truncation. If the current aggregate representation is exact, linear
 transport of each rank-one component is exact. The approximation enters through the initial
truncation and any later recompression, not through the transport itself.
• It is directly falsifiable. The stored teacher arrays can test the exact channels that matter: variance,
 diagonal d3, K4d, and the covariance entries feeding them.
• It is plausibly outside the measured dead list. It is not source-by-source storage, and it does not
 require a shared basis for all individual source legs. It is an aggregate representation of the
combined old contribution.
3. The important corrections
A. Rank-230 D21 error does not imply 2% channel error. A Frobenius-optimal rank-k approximation
minimizes ||D21−D21_k||_F, but the next-layer variance uses specific W-weighted quadratic queries. A
small global Frobenius residual can still concentrate in directions that matter strongly to those queries.
Therefore the decisive quantities are the actual channel errors, not D21 Frobenius error alone.
B. The d3 factor needs a convention check. If T = Σ σ_a u_a⊗u_a⊗v_a literally, then T_iii = Σ σ_a u_ai²
v_ai. A factor of 3 is not automatic. It is valid only if the stored representation is a specifically
symmetrized convention whose diagonal expands with that factor. Match this exactly to the teacher
definition before testing.
C. The nonlinear/ReLU recompression step is the key missing algorithmic detail. Linear transport can
preserve the factors, but after the ReLU/young-source join the aggregate old contribution must either
remain representable without rank growth or be deterministically recompressed. If recompression
requires forming the dense tensor, the claimed cost advantage disappears. The implementation
therefore needs an explicit factor-space update/recompression rule and its cost must be included.
D. The quoted cost needs normalization. For n=1024 and k=230, two n×n matvecs per component
require about 4.82×10^8 multiply/add-scale operations per layer. Relative to n^3 this is approximately
0.449 n^3, not 0.22 n^3. The ~400-neuron contraction is about 9.42×10^7 operations, approximately
0.090 n^3. Use the competition's exact unit convention when converting these counts to u. In any

case, the old-tier overhead is not enough by itself to establish a total ≤125u if the young tier remains
near 153u.
4. Correct channel formulas
For T_ijk ≈ Σ σ_a u_ai u_aj v_ak, the required D21 slice is:
 D21_i,c = Σ_a σ_a · u_ai² · v_ac.
The old contribution to the variance of output neuron i is:
 δVar_i = Σ_a σ_a · [w_iᵀ(u_a∘u_a)] · [w_iᵀ v_a].
The diagonal third cumulant must follow the exact teacher convention; under the literal unsymmetrized
tensor above:
 d3_i = Σ_a σ_a · u_ai² · v_ai.

mean-relevant channels. At layers 7, 11, 14, 15 and 16, report relative errors separately for diagonal
and off-diagonal quantities.

7. Recommended single falsifier
At layer 7, take the exact old-source aggregate from the teacher data. Construct its best rank-k
representation for k = 100, 150, 200, 230, 300. For each k, immediately evaluate the three quantities
that matter to the next-layer means: (i) the W-weighted variance correction for the active neurons, (ii)
d3, and (iii) K4d after the existing closure. Then run the same factor state forward without teacher
resets to layers 11, 14, 15 and 16. This single experiment simultaneously tests compression quality,
query alignment, and memory stability.

Interpretation: If k=230 passes the channel gate and the free-run errors remain within the chain's gate,
the representation is worth integrating. If it fails, the failure mode tells you what to fix: a variance-only
failure indicates query-misaligned compression; a depth-growing failure indicates rank
loss/recompression error; a d3/K4d failure indicates that D21 alone is not sufficient to reconstruct the
channels required by the closure.
8. Stage 2 target
Only after Stage 1 passes should the method be integrated into the chain. Measure final-layer mean
MSE against the 1e9-sample truth, paired with the chain, and then test across 100 MLPs on Kaggle.
Apply the supplied score thresholds exactly: MSE ≤ 1.07× chain at 102u, ≤ 0.95× at 115u, or ≤ 0.84×
at 125u. A prototype is plausibly useful at ≤0.8× chain score, but that is not the final target.
Final assessment
Verdict: the aggregated rank-k mechanism is the better Round-11 direction. Its central
insight—compress the aggregate long-memory third-cumulant content rather than each source leg—is
exactly the representation change the measurements suggest. But the claim that k=230 gives the
required channel accuracy is not established by a 2% Frobenius D21 result, and the total-cost claim
must be recomputed using the same u normalization as the chain. The next experiment should
therefore be a channel-aware, free-run rank sweep, not another argument from matrix rank alone.
