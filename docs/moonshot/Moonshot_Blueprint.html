3. The current method (what must be beaten)

The V29/V31 factorised K3 chain (504aldo, MIT) carries the mean, the covariance C and κ3 at every layer. κ3 is held as a sum over sources: every ReLU gives birth to a source with legs P and A = P·ab, where ab = diag(Φ)Coff.

  • The young tier (ages ≤ 4) keeps dense legs; older sources share a basis (rank 384 / 224); κ4 is memoryless (λ law).
  • Means and covariances come from the bivariate Wick/Edgeworth closure.

4. Error map: where accuracy is lost (K3ab–K3ad)

  • C1. Both closures are exact: given the true inputs, the mean closure is off by 9e-11. All error comes from the closure's inputs.
  • C2. Channel oracles (true value injected at every layer; reduction in final MSE): variance −47%, κ4 diagonal −30% (acts only through the mean closure), κ3 diagonal −14%, all three −94%.
  • C3. The error comes from the freshly born all-distinct part of κ3 at every layer, i.e. the post-ReLU κ3(hi,hj,hk).
    • That part is 9–16% wrong relative to itself, and it causes 91–96% of the D21 slice error.
    • The slice error grows from 4% to 7% with depth and is random (slope 1.00, R² 0.996).
    • The κ4 multi-index inputs add further error.
  • C4. Errors accumulate through the mean (gain 0.65–0.96 per layer) and through the covariance. The variance error is really the error of the off-diagonal post-ReLU covariance, with ~4.2e-5 of fresh error per entry per layer.

All deep-layer errors are well above the teacher's noise. The bar is severe: variance to ~0.1% and off-diagonal covariance to ~0.7%. This is why sampling fails and why κ3 must be carried at all. The weakest channel is the κ4 diagonal: 32–34% wrong at depth, with a systematic slope of 0.75–0.79 (the chain's κ4 is ~22% too small along the truth). Yet the κ4 constants test at their score optimum (C5), so the missing part is pattern, not scale.

C5. One-for-one law: every knob inside the chain (age gates, ranks, passes, screening, join rebuild, exact old tier, R_FB) trades ~1% of cost for ~1% of accuracy, in either direction. Examples:
  • exact old tier: MSE −3.9%, cost +19%
  • R_FB 128: MSE −3.1%, cost +43%
  • join transport: cost −6.6%, MSE +1–6%
The chain sits at its own optimum. Halving the cost is impossible inside this family.

C6. Covariance and closure cost only ~8u. Almost all the cost is carrying κ3. A Gaussian (κ2-only) chain is very cheap but ~10× worse (dropping the old tier alone: ×35).

C7. There is no temporal problem: the total κ3 moves as one object under one linear map, exactly. The problem is purely spatial: holding one dense n³ tensor cheaply.
C8. The mathematical wall (GPT 5.6 Sol): no linear representation of the total κ3 can be both smaller and closed under transport by every dense M. Sym³ is an irreducible GL(n) representation, so such a state needs ≥ C(n+2,3) ≈ 1.8×10⁸ numbers. Tucker, TT, CP, vector-metric, age modes and shared bases all hit this wall.
  • Our caveat: M is fixed and known, so the real bound is what future readouts can observe, ≤ n² per remaining layer. At mid depth this is close to the chain's ~3kn².
  • The lazy cubic circuit (store only birth matrices and M, read D21 by reverse mode) costs ≈ 245u, the same as the chain. The two are dual contraction orders of one tensor network.
Conclusion: changing how the tensor is factorised gains nothing. What is represented must change.

7. How old sources reach the final means (K3m)

  • C9. ~86% of the old-source slices' effect travels through mean shifts at the next 1–2 layers (variance → E[relu]). That path is near-range and full-rank; only ~14% goes through the low-rank final-variance path. Adjoint "keep only the relevant directions" is therefore dead.
  • C10. Dropping either the old D21 (n³) or the old d3 (diagonal, n²) at one late layer exceeds the error budget. The diagonal's weight grows toward the output.
  • C11. The Khatri–Rao wall: Hadamard products of factored legs have rank r(r+1)/2, which reaches n for r ≥ 45. In practice the spectrum is flat and noise-like, so sketching would need d ≈ 10⁸.

9. Checklist for any new proposal

  1. Does it hit the C8 wall, i.e. is it just another factorisation of the same κ3 tensor? If so, reject it.
  2. Where does the old-source content go (C9: sources 10+ layers old, ~88% coherent)? Dropping it costs 36–40% error (DHMP).
  3. Count the cost in n³ products: ≤ ~115u? (One dense 1024² product ≈ 0.5u with Strassen L5, so ~230 products = 115u.)
  4. Is there a one-layer falsifier on stored data? Teacher: pre/post mean, C, Y21 and κ4 diagonal at layers 3/7/11/14/15/16. Captures: every source at L7/11/14/15.
  5. Grader constraints: 8 GB RAM, float32, residual time < 0.4 s, only flopscope + whestbench + stdlib, no print.
